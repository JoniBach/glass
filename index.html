Glass

A design system for displays behind a two-way mirror, built for the agents that write to them. Black is the mirror, light is the only material, sizes come from viewing distance, and content arrives as a role and is trimmed to fit.

Read more: principles and sources · writing for the glass · Glass text reference. Everything below is generated from tokens/tokens.resolved.json and schema/roles.json.

Principles

  1. Black is the mirror. No backgrounds or fills; structure is type, spacing and hairlines.
  2. Light leaks, so spend it sparingly. Lit area stays under a budget; no filled shapes.
  3. Brightness tiers, not colours. Three inks; the faintest is for decoration only. One accent, for alerts.
  4. Type size comes from viewing distance. Room tier reads across the room; near tier at the mirror.
  5. A glance is under two seconds. The title is the message; limits are enforced, not suggested.
  6. Stay in the periphery. Slow fades, no loops; only alerts demand attention; failures fail quietly.
  7. The middle is the mirror. Content lives in bands; nothing ever clips; ambient content yields first.
  8. Written for agents. Roles in, escaped markup out, a headless check after every change.
  9. Agents write plain text. role: title blocks, compiled to JSON; the schema is the grammar.

Type

Ink

Hierarchy is brightness. Through 25% glass in a lit room, ink-3 is close to invisible, so nothing the reader needs may use it.

Roles and limits

Agents choose a role, never a position. Limits are per field, in words and lines; a line's length comes from the type size. Over-limit content is trimmed at a word boundary and the response lists what changed.
